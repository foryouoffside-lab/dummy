## 1. Overview & Search Architecture
The Digit Span Memory Test evaluates the operational storage capacity and rehearsal efficiency of the phonological loop within Alan Baddeley's multicomponent working memory model (Baddeley & Hitch, 1974). Originating from David Wechsler's standardized clinical batteries (Wechsler, 1945), this forward digit span test quantifies how many sequential numerical items an individual can actively maintain before decay or articulatory displacement occurs. In his seminal paper, George A. Miller (1956) documented the normative human span of 7 ± 2 items. However, modern neurocognitive research (Cowan, 2001) confirms that pure unchunked focal attention is strictly limited to 4 units; individuals exceeding this threshold rely on subconscious or deliberate chunking strategies, binding isolated digits into hierarchical phonological groups before retrieval.
