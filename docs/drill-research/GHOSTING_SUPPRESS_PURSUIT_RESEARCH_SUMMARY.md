## 2. Neurocognitive Grounding & Peer-Reviewed Sources

All sources verified with persistent DOIs and registered in `lib/drillSources.js`:

1. **Burr (1980)**: *Motion smear: Two types of visual suppression.* Nature, 284(5752), 164–165.  
   `https://doi.org/10.1038/284164a0`  
   *Core Finding:* Seminal psychophysics investigation demonstrating how active visual suppression mechanisms attenuate motion blur trails and smear artifacts during movement, preserving crisp object contours.
2. **Martinez-Conde, Macknik, & Hubel (2004)**: *The role of fixational eye movements in visual perception.* Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 5(3), 229–240.  
   `https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1348`  
   *Core Finding:* Elucidates the physiological role of microsaccades, ocular drift, and microtremor in maintaining foveal neural firing and visual target clarity without fading.
3. **Rolfs (2009)**: *Microsaccades: Small steps on a long way.* Vision Research, 49(20), 2415–2441.  
   `https://doi.org/10.1016/j.visres.2009.08.010`  
   *Core Finding:* Details how fixational microsaccades counteract retinal adaptation and dynamically adjust gaze placement during high-precision visual fixation tasks.
4. **Krauzlis (2004)**: *Recasting the smooth pursuit eye movement system.* Journal of Neurophysiology, 91(2), 591–603.  
   `https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00801.2003`  
   *Core Finding:* Analyzes the neural architecture linking visual target selection and foveal gaze hold with extraocular motor command generation.
5. **Leigh & Zee (2015)**: *The neurology of eye movements (5th ed.).* Oxford University Press.  
   `https://doi.org/10.1093/med/9780199969289.001.0001`  
   *Core Finding:* The definitive reference text on smooth pursuit gain maintenance, fixation hold mechanisms, and visual suppression of extraneous motion noise.
