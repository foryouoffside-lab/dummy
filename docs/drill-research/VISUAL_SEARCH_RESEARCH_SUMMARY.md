## 3. Psychophysical & Cognitive Foundations

1. **Feature Integration Theory & The Binding Problem (Treisman & Gelade, 1980):**
   Treisman established that while elementary features (orientation, line closure, color) are parsed in parallel across early visual cortex (V1/V2), binding multiple features into a single coherent percept requires focused spatial attention deployed sequentially across candidate items.
2. **Guided Search 2.0 (Wolfe, 1994):**
   Wolfe demonstrated that search in natural scenes is guided: preattentive feature channels compute top-down and bottom-up salience, constructing a priority map that guides the focal spotlight toward high-probability candidates.
3. **Distractor Homogeneity & Target Similarity (Duncan & Humphreys, 1989):**
   Duncan and Humphreys proved that search latency increases sharply when distractors lack homogeneity. Rotating distractors (e.g., 'O', 'Q', 'G') destroys preattentive background grouping, forcing serial focal scrutiny of individual cells.
4. **Perceptual Load & Attentional Focus (Lavie, 1995; Bacon & Egeth, 1994):**
   Lavie showed that high perceptual load (dense clutter under time pressure) consumes early sensory bandwidth, suppressing irrelevant distractions and enforcing selective focus.
5. **Attentional Zoom Lens (Eriksen & St. James, 1986; Woods et al., 2015):**
   Spatial attention functions as a variable-aperture zoom lens. Expanding the lens permits coarse multi-item grouping, while narrowing it resolves fine contours (such as the open gap of 'C' vs. closed loops).

## 5. Evidence-Based Training Protocols

1. **Parallel Preattentive Coarse Filtering (Wolfe, 1994):**
   Keep gaze slightly unmoored across rows to allow peripheral vision to eliminate clusters of completely dissimilar shapes simultaneously before deploying focal foveation.
2. **Saccadic Fixation Cadence (Treisman & Gelade, 1980; Woods et al., 2015):**
   Standardize an ocular motor rhythm executing 3–4 ballistic fixations per row, absorbing 3–4 characters per fixation dwell time (200–250 ms) rather than inspecting one item at a time.
3. **Feature Discrepancy Isolation & Distractor Suppression (Lavie, 1995; Duncan & Humphreys, 1989):**
   Prime visual attention for *open contour / gap detection* rather than the full target character. Closed continuous loops ('O') are inhibited early, surfacing broken arcs ('C') instantly.
4. **Structured Serpentine Sweeping (Bacon & Egeth, 1994; Eriksen & St. James, 1986):**
   Execute a disciplined serpentine raster scan across rows (left-to-right on row 1, right-to-left on row 2) to eliminate ocular backtracking and ensure 100% coverage without redundant saccades.
