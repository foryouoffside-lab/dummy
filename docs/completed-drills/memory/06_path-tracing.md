## 1. Overview & Scientific Foundation

The **Path Tracing Memory Test (Corsi Block-Tapping Test)** is the gold standard assessment of visuo-spatial sequential working memory. It measures the longest sequence of visuo-spatial steps that can be reproduced in exact chronological order. Unlike static matrix tests, path tracing directly engages the active "inner scribe" (Logie, 1995), measuring dynamic trajectory maintenance and motor sequence planning.

### Key Literature & Theoretical Foundations:
- **Philip M. Corsi (1972)**: *Human memory and the medial temporal region of the brain* – McGill doctoral dissertation establishing the block-tapping task.
- **Brenda Milner (1971)**: Demonstrated double dissociation between left temporal lesions (verbal span deficits) and right temporal/hippocampal lesions (spatial span deficits).
- **Robert Logie (1995)**: *Visuo-spatial working memory* – Delineated the active "inner scribe" from the passive "visual cache".
- **Kessels et al. (2000, 2008)**: Established adult normative benchmarks for the forward Corsi span (~5.4 ± 0.8 blocks).
- **Berch, Krikorian & Huha (1998)**: Neuropsychological and developmental analysis of the Corsi task.
- **Woods et al. (2015)**: Browser chronometry and hardware refresh quantization.
