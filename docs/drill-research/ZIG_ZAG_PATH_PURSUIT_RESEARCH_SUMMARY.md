## 2. Neurophysiological Mechanisms
Tracking along a multi-segment zig-zag polyline represents one of the most demanding ocular motor tasks:
1. **Multi-Vector Decomposition:** Continuous diagonal linear pursuit requires coordinated firing between horizontal oculomotor circuits in the pons (paramedian pontine reticular formation, PPRF) and vertical circuits in the midbrain (rostral interstitial nucleus of the medial longitudinal fasciculus, riMLF).
2. **Acute Vertex Reversals & Retinal Slip:** At each zig-zag inflection point, the velocity vector changes abruptly. This creates sudden spikes in retinal slip and positional error.
3. **Saccade-Pursuit Integration:** The brainstem and superior colliculus trigger an instantaneous catch-up saccade to re-center the fovea onto the target (de Brouwer et al., 2002; Krauzlis, 2004).
4. **Cerebellar Forward Internal Models:** Repetitive tracking calibrates predictive cerebellar control (Barnes, 2008), enabling gradual deceleration into vertices and rapid smooth pursuit re-acceleration along the exiting vector leg.
5. **Dynamic Visual Agility & Anti-Habituation:** Activating 'Hide Line' removes visual path cues, forcing reliance on real-time sensory feedback, while 'Random Speed' prevents mechanical anticipation.
