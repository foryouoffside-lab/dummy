## 2. Neurocognitive Grounding & Peer-Reviewed Sources

All sources verified with persistent DOIs and registered in lib/drillSources.js:

1. **Stark, Vossius & Young (1962)**: *Predictive control of eye tracking movements.* IRE Transactions on Human Factors in Electronics, HFE-3(2), 52–57.  
   https://doi.org/10.1109/THFE2.1962.4503342  
   *Core Finding:* The seminal engineering study on human tracking of sinusoidal target waveforms, demonstrating predictive phase lock and gain matching that eliminates reflex lag during rhythmic motion.
2. **Robinson (1965)**: *The mechanics of human smooth pursuit eye movement.* The Journal of Physiology, 180(3), 569–591.  
   https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1965.sp007718  
   *Core Finding:* Establishes mathematical negative-feedback dynamics of smooth pursuit, measuring open-loop acceleration and closed-loop velocity tracking capabilities.
3. **Rashbass (1961)**: *The relationship between saccadic and smooth pursuit-tracking eye movements.* The Journal of Physiology, 159(2), 326–338.  
   https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1961.sp006811  
   *Core Finding:* Proves independent neurological control loops for smooth pursuit (responding to target velocity/retinal slip) versus saccades (responding to positional error).
4. **Bahill, Iandolo & Troost (1980)**: *Smooth pursuit eye movements in response to unpredictable target waveforms.* Vision Research, 20(11), 923–931.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/0042-6989(80)90073-5  
   *Core Finding:* Compares predictable harmonic waveforms with non-harmonic signals, proving that human smooth pursuit achieves unity gain and zero phase lag primarily when waveform periodicity allows internal model construction.
5. **Barnes (2008)**: *Cognitive processes involved in smooth pursuit eye movements.* Brain and Cognition, 68(3), 309–326.  
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bandc.2008.08.020  
   *Core Finding:* Summarizes working memory and cerebellar internal forward models that predict periodic target oscillations, enabling predictive smooth pursuit.
