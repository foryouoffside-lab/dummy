## 1. Overview & Scientific Foundation

The **N-Back Working Memory Test** is the premier neuropsychological paradigm designed to assess and train dynamic working memory updating, executive control, and continuous information maintenance under high temporal pressure. Participants continuously evaluate whether the currently presented stimulus matches the one displayed exactly $N$ steps earlier, requiring active item eviction and queue updating.

### Key Literature & Theoretical Foundations:
- **Wayne K. Kirchner (1958)**: *Age differences in short-term retention of rapidly changing information* – Introduced the continuous N-back task.
- **Alan Baddeley & Graham Hitch (1974)** / **Baddeley (1986, 2000)**: Multicomponent working memory model; the central executive coordinates attention and buffer updating between phonological and visuo-spatial subsystems.
- **Adele Diamond (2013)**: *Executive functions* – Identified working memory updating, inhibitory control, and cognitive flexibility as the foundational triad of executive functioning.
- **Susanne M. Jaeggi et al. (2008)**: *Improving fluid intelligence with training on working memory* – Demonstrated significant transfer from adaptive dual N-back training to fluid intelligence ($G_f$) and matrix reasoning.
- **Nelson Cowan (2001)**: *The magical number 4 in short-term memory* – Formalized the fundamental 4-item limit of the focus of conscious attention.
- **David L. Woods et al. (2015)**: Browser chronometry and reaction time measurement standards.
