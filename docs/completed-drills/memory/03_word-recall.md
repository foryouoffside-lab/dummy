## 1. Overview & Scientific Foundation

The **Verbal Memory Test (Word Recall)** evaluates immediate free recall, semantic associative encoding, and verbal working memory span using progressive word lists. Unlike recognition paradigms, free recall requires internal cue retrieval, placing heavy demands on the phonological loop and central executive.

### Key Literature & Theoretical Foundations:
- **Ebbinghaus (1885)**: *Über das Gedächtnis* – The mathematical formulation of the forgetting curve and savings in relearning.
- **André Rey (1958)**: *L'examen clinique en psychologie* – The Rey Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT) evaluating verbal learning rate and interference dynamics.
- **Bennet B. Murdock Jr. (1962)**: *The Serial Position Effect of Free Recall* – Formalized the U-shaped recall curve delineating primacy and recency effects.
- **Craik & Lockhart (1972)**: *Levels of Processing: A Framework for Memory Research* – Demonstrated superior retention through deep semantic encoding over shallow phonemic rehearsal.
- **Woods et al. (2015)**: High-resolution millisecond browser chronometry and timing standards.
