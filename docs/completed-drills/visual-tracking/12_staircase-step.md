## 1. Overview & Search Architecture
Vertical eye movements are neuroanatomically segregated from horizontal tracking, relying exclusively on midbrain burst and tonic neural integration centers—specifically the rostral interstitial nucleus of the medial longitudinal fasciculus (riMLF) and the interstitial nucleus of Cajal. Physiological assessments demonstrate that vertical pursuit naturally exhibits lower velocity gain, longer reaction latencies, and elevation asymmetries compared to horizontal tracking. Staircase Step exercises vertical extraocular rectus muscles and midbrain burst firing across multi-tiered orthogonal ramps and vertical steps, requiring head immobilization, corner feedforward braking, and rapid elevation saccades.
